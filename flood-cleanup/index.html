Whether the water came from a storm, an overflowing river, a backed-up sewer, a failed sump pump or a burst plumbing line, flood damage needs a trained crew right away. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. Marietta Restoration handles emergency flood cleanup across Marietta and answers 24/7 to pump out the water, sanitize what it touched and restore your property safely.

Cleanup opens with rapid extraction: truck-mounted units and submersible pumps that pull thousands of gallons an hour out of the building. Next our technicians, who hold IICRC certification, judge the level of contamination and choose a sanitizing plan that covers HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial application and disinfecting each surface that got wet. Anything unsalvageable comes out and is hauled off correctly, including wet insulation, drywall that took in contaminated water and waterlogged carpet padding.

Once the space is extracted and sanitized, our team brings in commercial drying gear, namely low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers plus high-velocity air movers, so the structural components dry all the way through. We check moisture levels every day with calibrated meters and thermal imaging so the drying is complete and mold does not get a start. Your insurance adjuster will want scope-of-work reports, equipment logs, moisture maps and photographs, so we keep that documentation going through the whole project.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Elevate appliances and electrical systems in flood-prone parts of your property
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • Install a battery backup sump pump so you stay protected when storms knock out power
  • Check that grading on all sides of the house carries water away from the foundation
  • Roof runoff should leave the downspout far enough out that it cannot soak the foundation
  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Stains along baseboards and lower walls that mark the flood level
  • Heavy rainfall, a dead sump pump and water spreading across the basement
  • Water collecting along the foundation because the yard is saturated
  • Water flooding out of a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
  • Seepage at door thresholds, window wells or cracks in the foundation

Why Flood Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

There is much more to a flood cleanup than removing the water you can see. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Beyond the health concerns, a poor flood cleanup often leaves hidden moisture that turns into severe mold growth within days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. Nothing else reliably heads off secondary damage, and that damage can run thousands more to remediate, so we dry by the numbers.
