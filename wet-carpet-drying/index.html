Wet Carpet Drying Help in Marietta

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. Our team in Marietta extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Warning Signs

  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • Musty, sour odor that builds whenever the door and windows stay shut
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

How We Fix It

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Soaked Pad Underneath

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. Long after the top feels dry, that pad is still passing moisture up into the carpet and down into the floor.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Stains From Furniture

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.
